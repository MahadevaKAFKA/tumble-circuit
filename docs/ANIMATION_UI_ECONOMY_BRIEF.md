### 1. Make the racing feel fast

- Add a **free default speed trail** to every player's marble, available from the first race without equipping cosmetics. Fade it in above cruising speed and grow its visible length smoothly with actual movement speed. Use the existing speed cap as a reference. Initial test knots can be near zero below 15% of cap, around 4 studs at 35%, 12 at 65%, and 24 at cap. Interpolate and smooth, enforce a maximum, and tune after seeing the actual course. Use gentle hysteresis around the on/off threshold. Shorten and fade smoothly while braking. Use real speed, with a sanity cap and a short dropout reset, so a stationary spinning marble and a teleport never leave long ribbons.
- Use one pooled `Trail` per rendered marble where appropriate. On a spinning physics sphere, mount attachments to a movement-aligned visual follower or the existing presentation rig so the ribbon stays stable. Handle tiny velocities with the last valid direction. Verify scale, sampling, and streak placement at a sharp turn. Clear history on fall/reset, grid snap, teleport, finish, unequip, and camera target changes. Bound on-screen rival trails by distance and quality tier. Keep a brief outline of local motion on Low. Do not stream per-frame cosmetic updates through remotes. For a custom trail, replace or restyle the default effect through the same speed curve. Every player gets the same practical motion readability budget.
- Layer in a restrained speed-sensitive camera FOV, optional edge streaks at very high speed, a roll sound that rises with speed and surface, and short, distinct Boost/Shield/landing cues. Start with a modest 5–10° maximum FOV increase and smooth both directions to preserve steering precision. Keep the center and hazards legible on mobile. Reduced Motion removes the optional FOV kick, shakes, and cuts while preserving steering and core feedback.
- Keep the existing authoritative movement, speed cap, checkpoint order, input, and results mechanisms. Measure baseline input response, frame time and representative section splits before changing physics. Tune chase camera and visual cues first. If actual acceleration needs attention, propose a separate measured tuning pass with before/after brake distance and turn-control metrics. Preserve existing records unless the owner explicitly chooses a new leaderboard version after a physics change.

| 0.00–0.35 s | Show the spool queued on a curved launch track, with a strong central focus and clear rails. |

**Silhouettes and unnamed cosmetics.** Render each entrant from its actual outline family using neutral unlit material, an optional thin rim, and deliberately hidden color, patterns, textures, and catalog identity. The same shape shared by multiple skins produces the same mask across rarities. When two trail effects have the same geometry or motion profile, use the same neutral trace, with textures and particles masked. Keep the primary race reel shape-only with no fake names or filler words such as `Go` or `Wow`. Keep the result presentable with a rarity, category and real swatch/preview. **Outcome and UX.** The server selects and durably records the result and any currency debit or entitlement exactly once, and the client only plays the reveal after it has a confirmed outcome. Present the reel as a reveal, with the predetermined winner staged to cross first. A camera cut, timing, skip, input, or animation RNG cannot affect the reward.
